🌊 Essence of Hangul

Essence of Hangul — 훈민정음 (Hunminjeongeum)

Meaning: the proper sound for the instruction of the people

Sejong the Great succeeded in creating what was known in his time as Hunminjeongeum (훈민정음), meaning “the proper sound for the instruction of the people.”

Modern Hangul includes both vowels and consonants. Vowels start from three basic elements based on the philosophical concept of Yin and Yang:

  • Sky (·) — Yang: the dot, the sun in the sky
  • Earth (—) — Yin: the horizontal line, the flat earth
  • Human ( | ) — Neutral: the vertical line, the human

Yang symbolizes positive and active energy, like the Sun. Yang vowels make light and positive sounds. In the diagram, these are the Light Sounds (Yang vowels).

Yin symbolizes quieter, darker energy that feels more subtle and distant, like the Moon and the (hidden) Sun during the night. Yin vowels make dark and deeper sounds. In the diagram, these are the Dark Sounds (Yin vowels).

With the combination of Sky (·), Earth (—), and Human ( | ), the rest of the Korean vowels were born.

The 10 simple vowels and 14 simple consonants make up Hangul's 24 basic letters. This chapter covers the first six simple vowels: ㅏ a, ㅓ eo, ㅗ o, ㅜ u, ㅡ eu, and ㅣ i — their sounds, mouth shapes, and stroke order.

Simple Vowels 1 — The First Six Vowels

Korean: ㅏ ㅓ ㅗ ㅜ ㅡ ㅣ   Romanization: a eo o u eu i

In Hangul, there are 24 basic letters: 10 simple vowels and 14 simple consonants.

Simple vowels (10): ㅏ ㅓ ㅗ ㅜ ㅡ ㅣ ㅑ ㅕ ㅛ ㅠ
Simple consonants (14): ㄱ ㄴ ㄷ ㄹ ㅁ ㅂ ㅅ ㅇ ㅈ ㅊ ㅋ ㅌ ㅍ ㅎ

These 10 vowel sounds make the base for the pronunciation of all the syllables in Korean, which are combinations of vowels and consonants. Each Hangul vowel makes a specific sound.

This chapter meets the first six vowels: ㅏ a (like the a in father), ㅓ eo (like the beginning of awesome, without rounding the lips), ㅗ o (like the o in hole), ㅜ u (a bit like the oo in oof), ㅡ eu (mouth wide and flat, teeth together, lips not rounded), and ㅣ i (like the elongated i in ski).

Stroke order: ㅏ — vertical line top to bottom, then a short stroke to the right from the middle. ㅓ — short stroke to the right, then a vertical line top to bottom. ㅗ — short vertical line top to bottom, then a long horizontal line left to right underneath. ㅜ — long horizontal line left to right, then a short vertical line down from the centre. ㅡ — one horizontal line, left to right. ㅣ — one vertical line, top to bottom.

Simple Vowels 2 — The Final Four Vowels

Korean: ㅑ ㅕ ㅛ ㅠ   Romanization: ya yeo yo yu

After the first six vowels, here are the final four simple vowels: ㅑ, ㅕ, ㅛ and ㅠ. They look similar to ㅏ, ㅓ, ㅗ and ㅜ, but these vowels begin with a y sound.

All four begin the same way: start with a short and soft ㅣ, then glide into the base vowel. 1) ㅑ — ya — like the ya in yacht. ㅣ + ㅏ. 2) ㅕ — yeo — like the sound in young or yummy. ㅣ + ㅓ. 3) ㅛ — yo — like the yo in yoga. ㅣ + ㅗ. 4) ㅠ — yu — like the yu in eulogy. ㅣ + ㅜ.

That completes all 10 simple vowels: ㅏ ㅓ ㅗ ㅜ ㅡ ㅣ ㅑ ㅕ ㅛ ㅠ

Stroke order: ㅑ — 1) vertical line, top to bottom. 2) short horizontal stroke to the right (upper). 3) short horizontal stroke to the right (lower). ㅕ — 1) short horizontal stroke, left to right (upper). 2) short horizontal stroke, left to right (lower). 3) vertical line, top to bottom, on the right. ㅛ — 1) short vertical line, top to bottom (left). 2) short vertical line, top to bottom (right). 3) long horizontal line, left to right, underneath. ㅠ — 1) long horizontal line, left to right, on top. 2) short vertical line down (left). 3) short vertical line down (right).
